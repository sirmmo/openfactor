OpenFactor

An open source factory-building platform for the browser. The simulation is a deterministic Rust library, and the game is content that runs on top of it.

Play API reference Source on GitHub

Status

Early prototype. The deterministic simulation, five rule sets with puzzles, saves, browser-to-browser co-op and tools for AI agents work. There is no public API or in-game rule editor yet.

The one rule

state(t+1) = sim(state(t), commands(t))

The simulation has no clock, no I/O, no floats and no randomised containers. Anything unpredictable enters as a command. Co-op sync, replays and integrations are all planned around this rule. Every run of the same commands produces the same state hash, which the game shows in its top bar.

Rule sets and puzzles

A new game (N) starts with a choice of rules, then of how to play them: free play on the pack's own map, a sandbox where everything is unlocked and free, or a puzzle. Puzzles come in two kinds. A campaign carries on in one factory, one goal after another, for as long as you want to keep building. A round starts on a map of its own, often with a clock and only some buildings allowed, and starts afresh every try; the game keeps your best time for each.

RulesWhat it is
FactoryThe full chain from ore to automata, with power, oil and four levels. An eight-step campaign to 25 automata, and two timed rushes.
First stepsA small tutorial: a drill, belts, a furnace and a workshop, one idea per goal.
HarvestFarming. Farms need water through pipes; grain, apples and milk become bread, juice and cheese, packed into picnic baskets. Wood pays for building, through the sawmill.
Chip fabShort timed rounds on small maps: sand to silicon to wafers, copper to wire, then transistors, chips and boards on solar power.
NetworkNothing is dug. Requests come in through gateways and pass through parsers, routers, databases and renderers to become responses, on compute from server racks that overheat when packed together. Parsers reject malformed requests, which must be logged or they stall. Each layer of the factory is a layer of the stack. Deliberately more complicated than it needs to be, to show how much happens between a click and a page.

Your own rules… in the same dialog loads a .gbx pack from a file; a mistake in it is reported with its line. Saves remember their rules, so loading one switches to them, and a pack loaded from a file travels inside the save.

Playing

You start with a coal-fired smelting line feeding a depot and a little stock. Everything a depot receives joins the shared stock, which pays for buildings and research. Removing a building refunds it in full, but whatever it held is lost.

How the base pack works

Ground holds iron, copper, coal, stone or tungsten; water and oil are pumped into pipes. Plates and bricks become gears, wire and circuits, then steel, carbide and plastic, then motors and controllers. An assembly line combines those with bricks into automata, the end of the chain.

Every building, resource and research is listed below, with its cost, written out from the pack itself; the game shows the same text on each building's card.

SectionBuildings
LogisticsBelt and the twice-as-fast Express belt. Splitter: shares items between its front and sides. Sorter: pick a resource in its inspector and it goes straight on while everything else leaves by the sides. Tunnel: sends items to the next tunnel facing the same way within 5 tiles, past anything in between. Hoist and Chute: build one at each end of a shaft, up to 3 levels apart, with nothing between them. Items go in the first and leave from the front of the last. Pillar: holds up what is built above it. Depot: stores anything.
ExtractionDrill: slow, unpowered. Power drill: three times faster and the only way to cut tungsten. Pump on water, Derrick on oil, both feeding pipes.
ProductionCoal furnace: burns coal, needs no power, makes smog. Arc furnace: clean and fast, and the only source of steel and carbide. Workshop: basic parts, unpowered. Assembly line: fast, makes every part. Refinery: oil and water into plastic, with tar out of its left side. If tar backs up the refinery stops, so give it somewhere to go.
UtilitiesSteam plant: coal and water. Tar burner: turns the refinery's waste into power. Solar panel: small, free and clean. Power pole: connects anything within 5 steps. Pipe: carries water and oil between neighbours.

Several recipes can run in one machine. Left on Automatic, a machine runs the first it has inputs for; choosing one in the inspector makes it accept only that recipe's inputs.

Coal furnaces, steam plants and tar burners foul the air around them. Smog clears slowly by itself, but coal furnaces packed together stop until it thins, so spread them out or move to arc furnaces.

A square marker on a machine means trouble: red for a shortage on its network, amber for output with nowhere to go, grey for map conditions such as smog.

The simulation runs at 60 ticks per second. A tile is 256 position units long and belt items sit at least 64 units apart. At speed 16 a belt carries one item every 4 ticks, and at speed 32 one every 2.

Rules

Everything except belts is defined in a rule pack, written in the rule language of GlassBox, the engine behind SimCity (2013). OpenFactor follows the syntax used by citysim's .gbx scripts. The game's toolbar, counters and sprites all come from the base pack.

resource ore   name "Ore"    sprite 97
resource plate name "Plates" sprite 107 tint c8763c
layer oreField sprites 3 32  tint 6f8fb8

unit miner   name "Miner"   sprite 110 group "Mining" placeOn oreField
unit smelter name "Smelter" sprite 75  group "Making"
unit depot   name "Depot"   sprite 58  stores any

unitRule mine
    appliesTo miner
    rate 30
    map oreField in 1
    local ore out 1
end

patch oreField at 0 0 size 6 6 amount 100000
place miner 2 2 east

A unit runs the first of its rules that can start. Inputs are taken at the start and outputs given rate ticks later. It accepts what its rules take, up to twice the amount, and sends what they make through its front.

Rule statementMeaning
appliesTo <unit>The unit this rule belongs to. Defaults to the rule's name.
rate <n>Ticks from start to finish. Defaults to 1.
local <R> in|out <n>, or <R> in|out <n>Items taken from or made in the unit.
global <R> in|out <n>Taken from or added to a world-wide stock.
local <R> out <n> left|rightMakes items that leave from that side instead of the front.
network <R> in|out <n>Draws from or supplies the unit's connected network for that resource.
map <M> in <n>Taken from the layer at the unit's tile.
map <M> out <n> [radius <r>]Added to the layer around the unit.
map <M> atLeast|atMost|greater|less|is <n>The rule starts only when this holds at the unit's tile. test map … also works.
log [-timestamp=Time] <metric> <n>Adds to a metric each time the rule finishes. Declare it first with metric <id> [name "…"]. Metrics appear in the Production panel.

Declarations

StatementMeaning
belt <id> [name "…"] [speed <n>] [style <n>] [model <file>] [corner <file>]A belt tier. Belts of different tiers join up, and the slower one sets the pace. The built-in belt can be adjusted the same way.
resource <id> [name "…"] [sprite <n>] [tint <rrggbb>]Something units make, carry or share over a network.
layer <id> [name "…"] [sprites <n>…] [tint <rrggbb>] [haze <rrggbb> full <n>]A map layer. sprites and tint draw ground; haze draws a colour that thickens up to the value full.
unit <id> [name "…"] [sprite <n>] [tint <rrggbb>] [group "…"] [model <file>]A building. group is its toolbar section and model the name of its 3D model.
… placeOn <layer>It can only be built where that layer has a value. Repeat for alternatives.
… stores anyAccepts any resource without limit.
… routes splitPasses items to its front and sides in turn. Routing units can't have rules.
… routes filterThe resource chosen for it goes straight on and the rest leave by the sides. With nothing chosen, everything goes straight on.
… routes tunnel [range <n>]Sends to the next tunnel of the same unit facing the same way within range, or else out of its front.
… links <R> [reach <n>]Joins that resource's network. Two members connect when either one's reach covers the other, counted in grid steps. Using network in a rule links a unit automatically, with reach 0.

Descriptions

describe <id> "…" gives a belt, unit, research or resource the sentence players see on its card, in the inspector, in the research tree and in the catalogue above. Write what it is for; recipes, costs, placement and requirements are worked out from the rest of the pack and added automatically. Descriptions are cosmetic, so they don't change the state hash.

describe furnace "Smelts ore with coal. Needs no power, but fouls the air."
describe routing "Splitters and tunnels, to share lines and pass belts under each other."

Titles and puzzles

title "…" and summary "…" name a pack in the New game dialog. A puzzle block sets goals; every count starts from zero when the puzzle does, and the simulation itself checks them, so everyone in a shared game agrees on the moment it is solved.

puzzle gearRush
    name "Gear rush"
    about "A bare map, 120 plates and five minutes: deliver 100 gears."
    fresh
    stock ironPlate 120 gear 20
    place none
    only belt drill furnace workshop depot
    goal deliver gear 100
    limit 18000
end
goal deliver <R> <n>That many delivered to stores shared units. Also goal make (made by any building), goal stock (in the shared stock at once), goal metric <metric> <n> and goal research <id>. A puzzle needs at least one.
freshThe puzzle starts a new world: the pack's map, stock and opening buildings, except for whichever of patch, stock and place the puzzle gives itself (stock none and place none start empty). researched <id>… starts it with research done. Without fresh the puzzle carries on in the factory already there.
only <building>…Only these can be built while it runs.
limit <ticks>Time allowed; past it the puzzle is lost, though the factory keeps running.

Puzzles are offered in the order they are written, and a solved one offers the next.

Art

Sprite numbers count across the sprite sheet, row by row from 0. A pack uses the Tiny Factory sheet unless it names another: sheet onebit is Kenney's 1-Bit Pack (49 sprites per row) with a dark floor and belts drawn as cables, as in the Network pack; sheet farm is Tiny Farm (12 per row) with grass and wooden cart tracks, as in Harvest. A model can name a file in a folder: space, nature, survival, food, pets and industrial hold models from the kits credited below, as in model space/computer. Art never changes how a pack plays.

The game opens in 3D (V switches to the flat map, and the choice is remembered). Three more art options are for it:

resource … model food/breadItems of this resource travel as that model instead of a coloured box, tinted toward the resource's tint.
layer … decor 40% nature/tree_oak@0.9 …Scenery scattered over the layer's tiles where nothing is built: a model is picked per tile, @0.9 scales it, and the percentage is how many tiles get any (all of them without one). It goes last on the line and is tinted toward the layer's colour.
belt with no modelDrawn as a flat strip that bends toward whatever feeds it: a cart track with sleepers in Harvest, a cable in Network.

Levels

levels <n>How many levels can be built on, up to 8. The default is 1, a flat world.
span <n>How far, in grid steps, something above the ground can be from the nearest building on the level below. The default is 3. A building can't be removed while something above relies on it alone.
… routes up [range <n>], … routes down [range <n>]A lift. Sends to the next lift of the same unit straight up or down within range, as long as the shaft between is empty. The last one sends out of its front.
place <what> <x> <y> <dir> [count <n>] [level <n>]Places a starting building on a level.

Belts, tunnels and machine outputs stay on their own level, so belts can cross over each other. Map layers exist only on the ground: placeOn units must be built there, and map statements in a rule read and change the ground under the building at any height. Network reach counts a level as one step.

Costs and research

Buildings and research are paid from the shared stock. A unit with stores shared adds what it receives to that stock, which is also what global rules read and write.

stock ironPlate 150 gear 30
cost drill ironPlate 8 gear 3

research electricity
    name "Electricity"
    requires copperWorking masonry
    cost copperPlate 40 wire 40
    time 600
    unlocks steam pole arcSteel
end

unlocks takes belts, units and rule names, and whatever is named stays unavailable until that research finishes. One research runs at a time. A pack containing the single statement sandbox makes everything free and open.

Networks

A network resource never travels on belts. A producer holds what it makes, up to two batches, until something on the same connected network draws it. Producers are drawn from oldest first, and machines are served in the order they were built. Cutting or joining a network loses nothing; removing a producer destroys only what it held.

resource power name "Power"
unit pole  sprite 116 links power reach 5
unit plant sprite 109
unit arc   sprite 75

unitRule burn
    appliesTo plant
    rate 120
    local coal in 1
    network power out 60
end

unitRule smelt
    appliesTo arc
    rate 40
    local ore in 2
    network power in 6
    local plate out 2
end

Map rules

A mapRule changes map layers by itself, tile by tile, every rate ticks.

mapRule growGrass
    rate 200
    map soil atLeast 20
    map water in 10
    map grass out 5
end

mapRule clearSmog
    rate 20
    map smog in 1
end

On a given tile, the rule applies only if all its conditions hold and every in amount is available; otherwise that tile is unchanged. Because the map has no edge, each map rule needs a map … in, or a condition that only non-empty tiles can meet (atLeast or is above zero, or greater). A map rule can only use rate and map statements, and it changes only the tile it is on, so radius isn't allowed. A layer needs sprites or haze to be drawn.

Differences from GlassBox and citysim

Numbers are whole, rules fire at the end of their rate instead of being averaged per tick, and every resource, layer, metric and unit must be declared, so a typo is reported with its line number. Declarations are on one line in the pack itself. log adds its amount when a rule finishes, not every tick. zoneRule, agentRule, agent and options are not supported yet and are reported as errors. network, belt tiers, routing and side outputs are OpenFactor additions.

Rule changes alter the state hash, so players on different rules are detected as out of sync. Names and sprites don't.

Embedding the simulation

The openfactor-sim crate has no dependencies.

use openfactor_sim::*;

let pack = Pack::parse(include_str!("base.gbx"))?;
let mut world = World::new(&pack);

world.step(&pack.worldgen.starter);
println!("{:016x}", world.state_hash());

// A save is the whole state as bytes. Loading checks it was made with the
// same rules, and the loaded world continues exactly as the original would.
let bytes = world.save();
let copy = World::load(&pack, &bytes)?;
assert_eq!(copy.state_hash(), world.state_hash());

Playing together

In the Game panel (G), Host a game gives a six-letter code and an invite link. Friends open the link, or type the code under Join, and build in your factory with you; everyone sees where the others are pointing.

The host's browser runs the game. It steps the world and sends each batch of ticks, with the edits made in it, straight to every guest over WebRTC. Guests send their edits to the host and run the same ticks, so every copy stays identical; they compare state hashes once a second and fetch a fresh copy if they ever differ. A player who joins late starts from a save of the host's world. Browsers find each other through the free PeerJS broker; the game itself never passes through a server.

The host chooses the rules and the puzzle. A guest playing other rules switches to the host's automatically, and when the host starts a game with other rules, everyone reloads and reconnects with the same code.

Keep the host's tab open and visible: if it is hidden the browser pauses it, and the game pauses for everyone. When the host leaves, guests go back to their own factories, which were saved when they joined. Some strict networks block direct connections between browsers; there is no relay server yet to work around that.

AI agents (WebMCP)

The play page offers its controls to AI agents through WebMCP, the proposed browser API that lets a page give tools to an agent running in the browser. An agent can do what a player can and nothing more: it pays the same costs, is refused for the same reasons, and the page says what it did.

ToolWhat it does
openfactor_overviewTime, shared stock, research progress, building counts and buildings with problems, and the rules and running puzzle with its goals and clock. Read only.
openfactor_catalogueEvery building, research and resource with descriptions, costs, recipes and placement rules. Read only.
openfactor_lookGround, smog and buildings in an area of up to 48 by 48 tiles on one level. Read only.
openfactor_inspectOne building's status, recipe, contents and networks. Read only.
openfactor_buildBuilds one building or a straight row of them, reporting each as built or refused with the reason.
openfactor_removeRemoves everything in a rectangle and refunds it.
openfactor_configureChooses a machine's recipe or a sorter's resource.
openfactor_researchStarts a research.
openfactor_waitRuns the factory forward by up to 120 seconds and reports how the stock changed.

The tools are registered with document.modelContext, or with the older navigator.modelContext that polyfills provide. Browsers only offer the API on secure pages, so it needs the game served over HTTPS (or from localhost).

Roadmap

  1. Headless simulation with replay tests (done).
  2. Browser client with a build and render loop (first version live).
  3. Two browsers in one world, with late join and desync detection (first version live, browser to browser).
  4. GlassBox-style rule packs, five rule sets with puzzles, and packs loaded from a file (first version live); an in-game rule editor and the external API.
  5. WASM mods, then federation between worlds.
